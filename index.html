Opening balances

Enter the company's equity accounts as of January 1. Your work saves automatically.

Show explanations & concept notes
Why enter numbers manually here? These come from a prior-period balance sheet — the accumulated result of years of transactions we don't have. In every transaction you add below, values like APIC always calculate automatically. The opening fields are the only exception, and only because we're starting mid-story.
Common stock
A legal minimum per share set in the charter — not the market price. Usually $1–$5. Its only job: split issuance proceeds into Common Stock (par × shares) and APIC (everything above par).
The maximum number of shares the company decided to allow in its charter when it incorporated. No journal entry is ever made for authorized shares. Appears only as a disclosure on the balance sheet. → See balance sheet
Total shares given out in all prior years. The Common Stock dollar balance (par × shares) is calculated automatically — you provide the count, the app derives the dollars. → See T-account
Why is APIC an input here? In any real transaction, APIC is never something you figure out first — it is always what's left over after subtracting par value from the cash received. The only reason you're typing it in here is that this opening balance represents years of prior issuances we don't have the details for. Think of it as a number you're reading off last year's balance sheet, not calculating. For any stock issued this year, the app calculates APIC automatically — you will never enter it yourself. → T-account → Balance sheet
Treasury stock (opening)
What treasury stock is: Shares the company issued and then bought back. Now held by the company itself — not outstanding, no dividends, no votes. It's a contra-equity account: shown as a deduction in the equity section (in parentheses). Cost method: record what was paid, no par split needed. → See on balance sheet → See T-account
Reduces outstanding shares immediately. Outstanding = issued − treasury. → See share tracker
What the company paid to repurchase these shares. Appears as (140,000) — a deduction — on the balance sheet.
Used when reissuing treasury shares. Proceeds above avg cost → APIC. Proceeds below → reduce retained earnings.
Preferred stock
What preferred stock is: A separate class with a fixed dividend — paid first, before common gets anything. Its own par value, its own APIC (never mixed with common). Dividend formula: rate % × par × shares issued. Does not depend on company earnings. → See dividend calculator
Same concept as common par — legal minimum, used for the issuance split. Preferred par is often $100 because it doubles as the dividend base.
Fixed rate. Formula: rate % × par × shares issued. e.g. 7% × $100 × 5,000 = $35,000/yr. Does not change with company profits. → See dividend calculator
The maximum number of preferred shares the company set in its charter. Not recorded in any account — appears as a disclosure on the balance sheet only.
All preferred shares issued in prior years. Typically no treasury stock for preferred — all issued shares are outstanding.
Prior years only — same logic as APIC-common. Its own account, never mixed with APIC-common. New preferred issuances calculate APIC automatically. → See T-account
Cumulative: Skipped dividends become arrears — owed in a future year before common gets anything. Non-cumulative: Skipped and gone. Most preferred is cumulative. → See dividend calculator
Arrears are not a balance sheet liability — they're a note disclosure only. But when a dividend is declared, all arrears must be paid first. Enter years skipped before this year.
Retained earnings & company
All net income ever earned, minus all dividends ever paid. Increases with income, decreases with dividends. Not a cash account — it's accumulated ownership interest. → See T-account → See balance sheet
Appears on the balance sheet header.

Transactions

Add each transaction. They sort by date automatically — the financial math always processes in chronological order regardless of entry order. Every transaction shows what it affects and why immediately below it.

Note on prices: Enter price per share, not a lump total. If a problem gives you total cash received for a block of shares, divide total ÷ shares first to get the per-share price.

Share tracker

Every event that changes share counts is shown here in chronological order. Outstanding shares — the number used for common dividends — is always issued minus treasury. Stock splits multiply all share counts but do not change total equity value.

Dividend calculator

Every declared dividend fully worked out. Preferred is always paid first and uses shares issued. Common uses outstanding shares only — issued minus treasury. The share counts used in each calculation are shown explicitly so you can verify them.

Journal entries

Debits on the left (no indent), credits on the right (indented). Totals must match — if debits ≠ credits, the entry is wrong. The math behind each line is shown in gray below the account name.

T-accounts

The stockholders' equity accounts only — opening balances and every transaction posted in date order. Bold = opening balance. Blue = this year's activity. Cash is an asset account and is excluded here since we are focused on the equity section.

Balance sheet — equity section

The final stockholders' equity section, formatted for a real balance sheet. Updates instantly with every change. Note: a stock split changes share counts and par value but does NOT change any dollar amounts in the equity section.
